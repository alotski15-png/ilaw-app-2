## INTENTIONS

### Learning Competency & Curriculum Standards

**Content Standard:**
The learners demonstrate understanding of the interactions among living and non-living things in an ecosystem.

**Performance Standard:**
The learners shall be able to create a visual representation (e.g., food web diagram, poster, or digital presentation) showing the flow of energy and matter in a chosen local ecosystem.

**Learning Competency:**
Differentiate biotic from abiotic components of an ecosystem and describe the feeding relationships among organisms (S7LT-IIh-6).

**Learning Objectives:**
By the end of the session, the learners will be able to:
1. Classify components of an ecosystem as biotic or abiotic with 90% accuracy.
2. Construct a simple food chain showing the transfer of energy from producers to consumers.
3. Explain the importance of maintaining balance in an ecosystem through a short written reflection.

**(Indicator 1.1.2)** — *Apply knowledge of content within and across curriculum teaching areas*

---

### Learner Context

The learners are a mixed-readiness group of 42 Grade 7 students. Approximately 60% are visual and kinesthetic learners who respond well to hands-on activities and multimedia presentations. About 25% require additional scaffolding in reading comprehension, particularly with scientific terminology. The class has 5 learners identified with varying attention spans who benefit from chunked activities and movement breaks. Several learners come from coastal communities and have prior experiential knowledge of local ecosystems (fishing, farming), which serves as a valuable fund of knowledge. The class generally enjoys group work and collaborative discovery activities.

**(Indicator 3.1.2)** — *Use differentiated, developmentally appropriate learning experiences to address learners' gender, needs, strengths, interests and experiences*

## LEARNING EXPERIENCE

### Pre-Lesson (5 minutes) — *Setting the Stage*

**A. Routine Activities (2 minutes)**
- The teacher greets the class warmly and asks a learner to lead a short prayer.
- The teacher checks attendance using a "thumbs up/thumbs down" quick check-in for learner well-being.
- The teacher reviews classroom norms posted on the board: *"Respect every voice, raise your hand, listen with intent."*

**(Indicator 2.6.2)** — *Manage learner behavior constructively by applying positive and non-violent discipline to ensure learning-focused environments*

**B. Motivation / Hook (3 minutes)**
- The teacher displays a large, vibrant image of a local mangrove ecosystem on the screen (projector/Smart TV).
- **Teacher asks:** *"What do you see in this picture? Is it alive or not alive? Why do you think so?"*
- Learners share quick observations. The teacher writes responses on the board under two columns: "Living Things" and "Non-Living Things."
- **Transition statement:** *"Today, we will explore how these living and non-living things work together in a community called an ecosystem."*

**(Indicator 1.4.2)** — *Use a range of teaching strategies that enhance learner achievement in literacy and numeracy skills*

---

### Flow (45 minutes) — *The Learning Journey*

#### Phase 1: Making Objectives Clear (3 minutes)

The teacher presents the learning objectives on a slide and reads them aloud. The teacher explains:
- *"By the end of our session, you will be able to tell the difference between biotic and abiotic components, build a food chain, and reflect on why balance in nature matters."*
- The teacher uses the **"3-2-1 Bridge"** strategy: Learners write **3** things they think they know about ecosystems, **2** questions they have, and **1** metaphor for what an ecosystem is like.

**(Indicator 4.1.2)** — *Plan, manage and implement developmentally sequenced teaching and learning process to meet curriculum requirements and varied teaching contexts*

#### Phase 2: Guided Discovery — Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

**A. Concept Presentation (5 minutes)**
- Using a slide presentation with embedded short video clips (2 minutes total), the teacher introduces:
  - **Biotic components** — producers, consumers, decomposers (with real-life examples from the local community)
  - **Abiotic components** — sunlight, water, soil, temperature, air
  - **Feeding relationships** — food chains and food webs
- The teacher uses **dual-language scaffolding**: key terms are presented in English with brief Filipino explanations for clarity (e.g., *"Ang producer ay gumagawa ng sarili nitong pagkain, tulad ng halaman."*)

**(Indicator 1.6.2)** — *Display proficient use of Mother Tongue, Filipino and English to facilitate teaching and learning*

**B. Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
- The teacher distributes mini whiteboards to each group.
- **Quick Quiz:** The teacher shows images one at a time (e.g., a fish, sunlight, a rock, a mangrove tree, bacteria). Learners write "Biotic" or "Abiotic" on their whiteboards and raise them.
- The teacher provides immediate feedback, correcting misconceptions (e.g., *"Some learners wrote 'abiotic' for bacteria — remember, bacteria are living things! They are decomposers."*)

**(Indicator 5.3.2)** — *Use strategies for providing timely, accurate and constructive feedback to improve learner performance*

#### Phase 3: Collaborative Exploration — Group Activity (15 minutes)

**Activity: "Build Your Ecosystem"**

- The teacher divides the class into **7 heterogeneous groups** of 6 learners each (mixed ability levels, genders, and learning styles).
- Each group receives a **"Ecosystem in a Box" kit** containing:
  - Printed pictures of biotic components (plants, animals, decomposers)
  - Printed pictures of abiotic components (sun, water, rocks, soil)
  - String and arrow markers for food chain construction
  - A large manila paper or chart
  - Glue and markers

**Task:**
1. Sort the pictures into **Biotic** and **Abiotic** categories and paste them on the chart.
2. Arrange the biotic pictures to form a **food chain** (at least 4 organisms: producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → decomposer).
3. Label each organism with its role.
4. Prepare a **30-second explanation** of their food chain.

**Teacher's Role During the Activity:**
- The teacher **roves** around the room, monitoring group progress.
- The teacher uses **proximity and positive reinforcement** to keep learners on task: *"I love how Group 3 is taking turns placing the pictures. Great collaboration!"*
- The teacher provides **scaffolding questions** for groups that need help: *"What does this organism eat? What eats this organism?"*
- For fast finishers, the teacher poses an **extension question**: *"What would happen if one organism in your food chain disappeared?"*

**(Indicator 2.3.2)** — *Manage classroom structure to engage learners, individually or in groups, in meaningful exploration, discovery and hands-on activities within a range of physical learning environments*

**(Indicator 2.5.2)** — *Apply a range of successful strategies that maintain learning environments that motivate learners to work productively by assuming responsibility for their own learning*

#### Phase 4: Presentation and Higher-Order Thinking (10 minutes)

**Group Presentations (5 minutes)**
- Three groups are randomly selected to present their ecosystem charts and food chains.
- Each group has 1 minute to present.
- The teacher uses the **"Two Stars and a Wish"** feedback strategy:
  - *"Two Stars"* — Two things the group did well
  - *"One Wish"* — One suggestion for improvement

**Higher-Order Thinking Discussion (5 minutes)**
- The teacher projects a **challenge scenario** on the screen:
  > *"A local factory has been releasing waste into the river near our community. Using what you learned today, what do you think will happen to the organisms living in and around the river?"*

- Learners engage in a **Think-Pair-Share**:
  1. **Think** (1 min) — Write individual ideas
  2. **Pair** (2 min) — Discuss with a partner
  3. **Share** (2 min) — Selected pairs share with the class

- The teacher facilitates the discussion, guiding learners to connect the concept to **environmental awareness** and **stewardship** (cross-curricular connection to Values Education/ESP).

**(Indicator 1.5.2)** — *Apply a range of teaching strategies to develop critical and creative thinking, as well as other higher-order thinking skills*

**(Indicator 3.2.2)** — *Establish a learner-centered culture by using teaching strategies that respond to learners' linguistic, cultural, socio-economic and religious backgrounds*

#### Phase 5: Individual Work and Formative Assessment (7 minutes)

**"Ecosystem Exit Pass"**
- Each learner receives a half-sheet of paper with 3 questions:

  **1. Classify:** List two biotic and two abiotic components found in a garden ecosystem.
  
  **2. Construct:** Draw a simple food chain with at least 3 organisms.
  
  **3. Reflect:** In 2-3 sentences, explain why it is important to protect the balance in an ecosystem.

- **Differentiation Option:** Learners who struggle with writing may draw and label their food chain and record a voice memo explaining their reflection (using a classroom tablet or phone with teacher permission).

**(Indicator 5.1.2)** — *Design, select, organize and use diagnostic, formative and summative assessment strategies consistent with curriculum requirements*

---

### Post-Lesson (5 minutes) — *Closing and Connection*

**A. Synthesis (3 minutes)**
- The teacher calls on 2-3 learners to share one thing they learned.
- The teacher summarizes key takeaways using the **"Ecosystem Equation"** on the board:
  > **Biotic + Abiotic + Interactions = Ecosystem**

**B. Preview of Next Lesson (1 minute)**
- *"Next time, we will explore how human activities affect ecosystems and what we can do to help protect them."*

**C. Closing Ritual (1 minute)**
- The teacher thanks the class for their active participation.
- A learner leads a short "thank you" prayer or reflection.

**(Indicator 2.4.2)** — *Maintain supportive learning environments that nurture and inspire learners to participate, cooperate and collaborate in continued learning*
